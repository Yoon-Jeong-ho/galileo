# Machine Generated Product Advertisements: Benchmarking LLMs Against Human Performance

- Year: 2024
- Venue: arXiv
- Authors: Sanjukta Ghosh (et al.; see arXiv record)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19610
- BibTeX key (if we add it): ghosh2024machine
- Tags: persuasion, marketing, evaluation, generation

## One-sentence takeaway

A small benchmark study comparing several LLMs vs humans on e-commerce product-description generation across a bundle of heuristic metrics (sentiment/readability/“persuasiveness”/SEO/etc.), finding GPT-4 strongest while smaller/older models often produce incoherent descriptions.

## What problem does it solve?

- Evaluates whether model-generated product advertisements/descriptions are comparable to human-written ones for e-commerce content creation.
- Provides an empirical comparison across multiple models and multiple “quality” dimensions beyond simple fluency.

## What is the core method / protocol?

- Collect 100 products.
- Generate product descriptions using four AI models: Gemma 2B, “LLAMA” (unspecified version in abstract), GPT-2, and ChatGPT-4.
- Generation is done in two conditions: with and without providing sample descriptions (few-shot / priming with examples).
- Compare against human-written descriptions.
- Evaluate with a “multifaceted evaluation model” consisting of:
  - sentiment
  - readability
  - persuasiveness
  - SEO
  - clarity
  - emotional appeal
  - call-to-action effectiveness

(From the abstract; details of metric implementations would need the PDF/HTML for exact definitions.)

## What are the key metrics?

- Sentiment
- Readability
- Persuasiveness
- Search Engine Optimization (SEO)
- Clarity
- Emotional appeal
- Call-to-action effectiveness

## What are the main results?

- ChatGPT-4 performs best overall under their evaluation.
- Other models (Gemma 2B, LLaMA variant, GPT-2) show “significant shortcomings” per authors, including:
  - incoherent / illogical output
  - weak logical structure and contextual relevance
  - losing focus on the product; disjointed sentences

## How is this similar to GALILEO?

- Broadly related in that it studies LLM-generated text quality and attempts to evaluate “persuasiveness” and other user-facing outcome proxies.
- Highlights practical evaluation dimensions (clarity/CTA/emotional appeal) that can inform how we structure human/automatic evaluation axes.

## How is this different from GALILEO?

- Task domain is e-commerce product descriptions/ads (not scientific/technical writing).
- Evaluation appears largely heuristic/feature-based rather than grounded in task-specific success metrics or controlled user studies (unclear from abstract).
- Focuses on model-vs-human comparison for a single generation task rather than GALILEO’s end-to-end pipeline goals.

## Where GALILEO is stronger / cleaner (if true)

- If GALILEO uses domain-grounded metrics, stronger baselines, and clearer experimental controls, it will be more convincing than a bundle of loosely defined “quality” scores.

## Where GALILEO is weaker / needs to improve

- If GALILEO lacks explicit “persuasion”/CTA/emotional tone measures, this paper is a reminder that stakeholders may care about these axes (even if we operationalize them differently).
