## One-sentence takeaway

ReviseQA proposes a multi-turn logical-reasoning benchmark where the context is incrementally edited (facts/rules added or removed) to test whether LLMs correctly *revise* conclusions rather than drift or cling to outdated premises.

## What problem does it solve?

- Static QA scores don’t measure whether models can *update* beliefs when earlier information is retracted or new constraints arrive across turns.
- In interactive settings (tutoring, legal, etc.), the agent must maintain logical consistency while re-evaluating conclusions under evolving context.

## What is the core method / protocol?

- A conversation-like sequence of turns.
- Each turn modifies the previous context by **adding** or **removing** facts and rules.
- The model must answer a query each turn, ideally recomputing the logically entailed conclusion given the updated knowledge base.
- Emphasis: belief revision under *explicit context edits* (not merely longer context).

## What are the key metrics?

(Only high-level metrics are described in the accessible abstract.)

- Per-turn correctness / logical validity of the conclusion.
- Multi-turn **logical consistency** under revisions (e.g., does the model retract prior conclusions when their support is removed?).
- Implicitly: revision success rate under additions vs retractions.

## What are the main results?

- The authors report that current LLMs “often fail to maintain logical consistency when updating beliefs” in this multi-turn revision setting.

## How is this similar to GALILEO?

- Both are about **multi-turn stability**: performance is not a single-shot score but a trajectory under sequential perturbations.
- The “revision vs drift” distinction is directly relevant to GALILEO-style robustness evaluations.

## How is this different from GALILEO?

- ReviseQA focuses on *formal logical reasoning* under explicit add/remove operations on facts/rules.
- GALILEO (as positioned by our related-work focus) is closer to **social pressure / persuasion / sycophancy** dynamics and recovery/time-to-failure style robustness.

## Where GALILEO is stronger / cleaner (if true)

- GALILEO can target *behavioral robustness* under social influence and measure failure/recovery dynamics that are closer to real adversarial conversations.

## Where GALILEO is weaker / needs to improve

- GALILEO should be careful to include controls that separate:
  - evidence-driven belief revision
  - from ungrounded drift / susceptibility / compliance.
- ReviseQA’s explicit add/remove protocol is a crisp control paradigm we can borrow.
