# Earth AI: Unlocking Geospatial Insights with Foundation Models and Cross-Modal Reasoning

- Year: 2025
- Venue: arXiv
- Authors: Tomer Shekel et al. (Google Research; large author list)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18318v1
- BibTeX key (if we add it): earthai2025shekel
- Tags: geospatial, remote-sensing, foundation-model, multimodal, agentic, crisis-response

## One-sentence takeaway

Earth AI is a Google GeoAI stack combining multiple domain-specific geospatial foundation models (imagery, population, environment) with a Gemini-powered reasoning agent, evaluated on benchmarks including multi-step real-world crisis scenarios.

## What problem does it solve?

- Bridging heterogeneous geospatial data sources (satellite/aerial imagery, environmental signals, demographic/population data) into end-to-end question answering and decision support without bespoke pipelines per task.
- Enabling complex, multi-step “analyst-style” queries that require orchestrating models + tools + data sources (rather than a single predictor).

## What is the core method / protocol?

- Build/assemble a *family* of geospatial FMs across three domains:
  - **Imagery**: “Remote Sensing Foundations” vision backbones + multimodal models; claims include open-vocabulary detection and cross-modal retrieval.
  - **Population**: “Population Dynamics Foundations” integrating human behavior/location signals.
  - **Environment**: models for weather/air quality/climate/disaster-related signals.
- Add a **Gemini-powered geospatial reasoning agent** that:
  - decomposes user questions into steps,
  - selects among Earth AI models and geospatial tools/data sources,
  - synthesizes outputs into an answer (positioned as “agentic reasoning over geospatial tools + FMs”).
- Evaluation includes a new benchmark of crisis-response scenarios (multi-step, real-world flavored tasks).

## What are the key metrics?

- Task/benchmark metrics vary by sub-domain; from the paper’s abstract/intro, highlighted capabilities include:
  - open-vocabulary object detection (likely mAP-type metrics),
  - zero-shot cross-modal retrieval (recall@k-type metrics),
  - crisis benchmark: scenario-level success/utility judgments (details later in paper).

## What are the main results?

- Claims (from abstract/intro):
  - domain FMs are competitive/SOTA on their respective benchmarks;
  - combining them yields complementary gains (“synergies unlock superior predictive capabilities”);
  - the Gemini agent can deliver “critical and timely insights” on crisis scenarios by jointly reasoning over models + data.

## How is this similar to GALILEO?

- Frames GeoAI progress around **foundation models for Earth observation** and emphasizes generality across tasks.
- Uses **representation learning + scaling** to unify multiple remote-sensing tasks and modalities.
- Highlights the value of **tool-augmented / agentic workflows** for geospatial analysis (not just single-shot inference).

## How is this different from GALILEO?

- System-of-systems orientation: explicitly integrates **multiple domain models + an LLM agent**, rather than “one FM to rule them all”.
- Strong emphasis on **population + environment** alongside imagery; many EO FMs focus primarily on imagery.
- The contribution is as much **product/system orchestration + benchmarks** as it is a single new architecture.

## Where GALILEO is stronger / cleaner (if true)

- If GALILEO’s pitch is a single coherent FM + reproducible training/eval: it may be easier to describe, reproduce, and position scientifically than a large integrated stack.
- If GALILEO is open / reproducible: can be more useful for the community than an integrated proprietary toolchain.

## Where GALILEO is weaker / needs to improve

- Need to articulate how GALILEO composes with **LLM agents and external geospatial tools** for multi-step analyst workflows (Earth AI makes this a headline feature).
- Need a story for **cross-domain fusion** (imagery + population + environment) or a clear rationale for not doing it.
