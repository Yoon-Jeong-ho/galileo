## 1) What problem does it study?
How to evaluate *political ideology* of LLMs beyond a single “left vs right” label, by measuring **topic-conditional stance stability**: does a model hold its stated stance when subjected to **argumentative pressure** (supporting/counter arguments, prompt/persona perturbations, or post-hoc fine-tuning)?

## 2) Experimental setup (what is being measured?)
- Task(s): open-ended responses to political statements (economic axis) + stance classification (agree/disagree) via an NLI model.
- Perturbation/pressure type:
  - “Stress tests” with **supporting arguments** and **counter-arguments** appended to the same statement.
  - Additional controllability stressors: opposite persona instructions; post-hoc right-leaning alignment via DPO.
- Multi-turn? **Sort of** (multiple *separate prompts* per topic: baseline + support + counter). Not a single chat trajectory.
- Metrics:
  - Core label set per model×topic: **Stable-Left / Unstable-Left / Stable-Right / Unstable-Right**.
    - Stability = whether stance persists between baseline and argument condition.
    - Left/right is defined via an *annotated bias direction* for each statement (economic axis polarity).
  - Topic-wise stability score: fraction of argument-sets that yield **stable** labels (computed over 3 independently generated argument sets).
  - Validation vs white-box uncertainty: correlation of stability with **Semantic Entropy** (AUROC reported up to **0.78** for Stable vs Unstable discrimination).
  - “Ideology reversal analysis” outcomes across conditions:
    - Stability-faithful (SF): only one-direction stable stance appears across conditions.
    - Stability-unfaithful (SU): stable stances of both orientations appear (full reversal).
    - Indeterminate (ID): no stable stance (only unstable).

## 3) Key findings (bullet)
- **Topic dependence is large**: a model’s global leaning does *not* predict topic stance; even “left-leaning models” output right stances on **27.6%** of topics (and vice versa for right-leaning models: left stances on **34.3%**).
- **Instability is common** within the majority ideology side (substantial mass on unstable-left/unstable-right), so reporting only a left/right label hides a robustness dimension.
- A simple black-box stress test (baseline + support + counter) produces stability labels that align with uncertainty: **Semantic Entropy** discriminates Stable vs Unstable with **AUROC up to 0.78**.
- **Stability predicts controllability**: under persona-flip prompting and even DPO right-alignment, models mostly remain “stability-faithful” (stable topics resist reversal; unstable topics are more malleable).

## 4) Limitations / threats
- Only **19** topics (Political Compass) and only the **economic left–right axis**; limited multidimensional coverage.
- Binary agree/disagree stance formalism; less directly applicable to domains without clear polarity.
- Not a true multi-turn dialogue process: pressure is applied via separate prompts (no within-conversation cumulative drift / recovery trajectory).
- Reliance on an NLI stance classifier to map open-ended text → agree/disagree.

## 5) How it relates to GALILEO
- What we can cite it for:
  - A clean framing of **stability under stress** as a first-class evaluation target (beyond mean bias).
  - A concrete, auditable way to generate “pressure operators” (support vs counter arguments) and report **topic-conditioned robustness**.
  - Using **uncertainty (semantic entropy)** as a validation signal for instability.
- Where we differ (our delta):
  - GALILEO is about **multi-turn robustness under social pressure**, *time-to-failure*, and **recovery after flip**; PReSS focuses on topic-level stability under a small set of argument probes.
  - GALILEO’s key causal desideratum is separating **evidence-driven revision** vs **pressure-driven drift**; PReSS does not explicitly include an evidence/control separation.
- Direct mapping:
  - Survival ↔ (not used; closest is their topic-wise stability score across a small number of stress probes)
  - TOF ↔ (not used)
  - Recovery ↔ (not used; their “ideology reversal” analysis is about controllability across conditions, not recovery after being misled)
  - Neutral Re-asking Control ↔ (their baseline vs support/counter re-asking is adjacent, but not a neutral re-ask after a flip)
