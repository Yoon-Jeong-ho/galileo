# MATRIX: Multi-Agent simulaTion fRamework for safe Interactions and conteXtual clinical conversational evaluation

- BibTeX key (if we add it): matrix2025
- Tags: multi-turn, clinical-dialogue, safety-evaluation, patient-simulation, llm-judge, hazard-taxonomy

## One-sentence takeaway

MATRIX is a safety-engineering-grounded framework that uses scenario/hazard taxonomies plus an LLM judge and an LLM simulated patient to run scalable, regulator-aligned safety evaluations of clinical dialogue agents.

## What problem does it solve?

- Typical clinical dialogue evaluations emphasize task success, correctness, or fluency, but miss interaction-level hazards and safety behaviors needed in safety-critical healthcare deployments.
- Human expert safety review is expensive and hard to scale/reproduce; existing simulators/judges often lack validated realism or hazard-detection reliability.

## What is the core method / protocol?

- A structured safety test library derived using safety engineering methods (inspired by SACE and SHARD/FMEA-style analysis):
  - “Patient input types” (interaction scenario categories)
  - “Expected behaviors” (what the system should do to be safe)
  - “Hazardous scenarios / failure modes” (ways the system can deviate and cause harm)
- PatBot: an LLM-based simulated patient conditioned on scenario/behavior pairs to generate diverse multi-turn conversations.
- BehvJudge: an LLM-based evaluator that reads the dialogue transcript and outputs a hazard pass/fail verdict with rationale; validated against clinician annotations.
- Experiments:
  - Exp 1: Validate BehvJudge on HazMAT (240 synthetic dialogues; 80 safe + 160 hazardous).
  - Exp 2: Assess PatBot realism and behavioral fidelity (incl. human factors review and a patient preference study).
  - Exp 3: Benchmark multiple LLM agents over large-scale simulated dialogues (reported as 2,100 dialogues, 14 hazard scenarios, 10 clinical domains).

## What are the key metrics?

- Hazard detection: F1 / sensitivity / precision / specificity (BehvJudge vs clinician labels).
- Domain-wise performance variation (across clinical specialties).
- Patient simulation realism / coherence / naturalness (qualitative + quantitative; details depend on their human factors and preference study design).

## What are the main results?

- LLM judges can match or exceed clinician hazard-detection performance on their HazMAT evaluation setting:
  - BehvJudge with Gemini 2.5 Pro: F1 ≈ 0.96 and sensitivity ≈ 0.999 (reported).
  - Clinician benchmark: F1 ≈ 0.94, sensitivity ≈ 0.95 (reported).
- PatBot realism analysis suggests LLM patient simulators can produce responses judged realistic/coherent (they report Llama-3.3-70B as most coherent/natural among tested patient models).
- Framework supports scalable evaluation: benchmarking 5 LLM agents over ~2,100 simulated dialogues across multiple hazard types and clinical domains.

## How is this similar to GALILEO?

- Shared theme: multi-turn robustness/safety evaluation with structured scenario coverage and metrics beyond single-turn accuracy.
- Uses automated evaluation (LLM-as-judge) to scale assessment, similar in spirit to automated multi-turn robustness harnesses.
- Explicitly models “drift”/failure over dialogue trajectories (hazards can emerge due to conversational mismanagement, not just wrong facts).

## How is this different from GALILEO?

- Domain-specific: focused on clinical history-taking and regulator-aligned safety engineering (ISO 14971 / SaMD framing), rather than general multi-turn manipulation/pressure/consistency.
- The evaluation target is interaction safety behaviors (red flags, escalation, refusal, clarifying questions), not primarily belief stability / sycophancy / agreement robustness.
- Adds a dedicated patient simulator (PatBot) and a hazard taxonomy produced via safety engineering workshops.

## Where GALILEO is stronger / cleaner (if true)

- If GALILEO targets general-purpose conversational robustness, it may offer broader, domain-agnostic coverage (beyond a clinical domain and its specific hazards).
- If GALILEO emphasizes longitudinal/trajectory metrics (time-to-failure, recovery), it can complement MATRIX-style binary hazard pass/fail with richer dynamics.

## Where GALILEO is weaker / needs to improve

- Safety-engineering alignment: GALILEO could borrow MATRIX’s structured hazard analysis and “expected behaviors” framing to make evaluations more audit-friendly.
- Realistic counterpart simulation: MATRIX invests in patient-simulator realism validation; GALILEO may need analogous validation for its “user/attacker/pressure” simulators.
