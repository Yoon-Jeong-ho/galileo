# CFBench: A Comprehensive Constraints-Following Benchmark for LLMs

- Year: 2024
- Venue: arXiv
- Authors: Tao Zhang; Chenglin Zhu; Yanjun Shen; Wenjing Luo; Yan Zhang; Hao Liang; Fan Yang; Mingan Lin; Yujing Qiao; Weipeng Chen; Bin Cui; Wentao Zhang; Zenan Zhou
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01122
- BibTeX key (if we add it): cfbench2024zhang
- Tags: constraints, instruction-following, benchmark, evaluation

## One-sentence takeaway

CFBench is a 1,000-example, real-scenario benchmark (Chinese) that evaluates multi-constraint instruction following via checklist-style decomposition, constraint-type taxonomy, and requirement-priority-aware scoring.

## What problem does it solve?

- Existing instruction/constraint-following evaluations are fragmented (single constraint types, narrow scenarios) and don’t reflect user-perceived satisfaction in realistic multi-constraint instructions.
- Scoring is often brittle because it ignores that some requirements matter more than others (priority).

## What is the core method / protocol?

- Dataset: 1,000 curated samples covering 200+ real-life scenarios, 20 domains, 50+ NLP tasks.
- Constraint framework: a taxonomy with 10 primary constraint categories and 25+ subcategories; constraints are “seamlessly integrated” into natural instructions (not appended as artificial checklists).
- Evaluation protocol (user-view):
  - Decompose each instruction into multiple checkpoints (a checklist).
  - Annotate each checkpoint with (a) constraint type and (b) requirement priority.
  - Use an LLM judge to evaluate checkpoint satisfaction, then aggregate with multi-dimensional criteria (constraints / instruction adherence / priority fulfillment).

## What are the key metrics?

- The paper describes a “multi-dimensional evaluation criteria” with three perspectives:
  - constraints perspective
  - instruction perspective
  - requirement-priority perspective

(Concrete formulas weren’t captured in this rapid pass; see paper Section 3+ / Appendix for the exact aggregation.)

## What are the main results?

- Across “leading LLMs”, CFBench finds substantial room for improvement on comprehensive constraints following.
- The paper additionally analyzes influencing factors and discusses enhancement strategies (details not fully extracted in this rapid pass).

## How is this similar to GALILEO?

- Shares the motivation of evaluating *instruction compliance* in more realistic settings (multi-part requirements, multiple “axes” of success).
- The checklist decomposition idea is analogous to turning a qualitative spec into multiple measurable pass/fail (or graded) criteria—useful for designing robust evaluation harnesses.

## How is this different from GALILEO?

- CFBench targets general constraint-following (format, content constraints, etc.), largely single-turn, and is benchmark-centric.
- GALILEO (as framed in our related-work goals) is more about robustness/failure modes over interaction (e.g., drift, misleading pressure, recovery), not just satisfying static constraints.

## Where GALILEO is stronger / cleaner (if true)

- If GALILEO operationalizes temporal/interactive robustness (time-to-failure, recovery, etc.), it addresses failure modes CFBench is not designed to measure.

## Where GALILEO is weaker / needs to improve

- GALILEO may lack a clear, human-facing taxonomy of “requirement types” and a priority-aware scoring scheme; CFBench provides a concrete template for that.
