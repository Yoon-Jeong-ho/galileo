# WavBench: Benchmarking Reasoning, Colloquialism, and Paralinguistics for End-to-End Spoken Dialogue Models

- Year: 2026
- Venue: arXiv (cs.CL)
- Authors: Yangzhuo Li, Shengpeng Ji, Yifu Chen, Tianle Liang, Haorong Ying, Yule Wang, Junbo Li, Jun Fang, Zhou Zhao
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12135
- BibTeX key (if we add it): wavbench2026li
- Tags: spoken-dialogue, multi-turn, benchmark, paralinguistics, evaluation

## One-sentence takeaway

WavBench is a tripartite benchmark for end-to-end spoken dialogue models that jointly tests (i) harder “reasoning” conversations, (ii) spoken-colloquial “listenability,” and (iii) explicit+implicit paralinguistic handling.

## What problem does it solve?

- Existing spoken-dialogue evaluations often inherit text-generation metrics and miss *audio-native* qualities (paralinguistics; background sound; natural colloquial speech) and/or don’t stress-test reasoning-heavy interactions.
- Prior benchmarks may target perception-only (understanding) or non-E2E pipelines rather than end-to-end speech-in/speech-out agents.

## What is the core method / protocol?

- Proposes **WavBench** with three subsets:
  - **Pro**: higher-difficulty tasks meant to stress reasoning-enhanced spoken agents.
  - **Basic**: a colloquialism standard focused on *listenability* (lexical appropriateness, linguistic naturalness, interactive rapport) rather than “written” correctness.
  - **Acoustic**: evaluates paralinguistics with both **explicit** instruction following (understanding/generation of emotion, accent, etc.) and **implicit** dialogue (responding appropriately to user tone/context).
- Evaluates 5 end-to-end spoken dialogue models (paper provides comparative analysis; details in full text).

## What are the key metrics?

- Multi-axis scoring across:
  - reasoning/task success in Pro;
  - colloquial quality / listenability dimensions in Basic;
  - paralinguistic understanding + generation + implicit alignment in Acoustic.
- (From intro): emphasizes whether agents can communicate multi-step reasoning clearly (reduce cognitive load) *and* sound natural.

## What are the main results?

- Across the tested models, performance varies substantially by axis; the benchmark surfaces tradeoffs between reasoning, colloquial delivery, and paralinguistic fidelity.
- Reported qualitative/quantitative findings suggest end-to-end models better capture paralinguistics than cascaded setups, but still struggle to unify reasoning with natural, audio-grounded delivery.

## How is this similar to GALILEO?

- Shares the theme of **evaluation protocol design** for *realistic multi-turn interaction* rather than single-turn/text-only scoring.
- Highlights that robustness/quality is multi-dimensional (content + interaction style + latent cues), analogous to GALILEO-style concerns about trajectory behavior over turns.

## How is this different from GALILEO?

- Domain focus is **spoken dialogue** (audio in/out, paralinguistics), not primarily conversational robustness to persuasion/sycophancy/multi-turn adversaries.
- The “robustness” notion is more about naturalness and acoustic alignment than about resisting drift/pressure or recovering to truth.

## Where GALILEO is stronger / cleaner (if true)

- If GALILEO targets *behavior under pressure/drift* with clear multi-turn perturbation protocols and recovery metrics, it is more directly aligned to robustness-for-safety questions than audio-quality benchmarks.

## Where GALILEO is weaker / needs to improve

- If GALILEO stays text-only, it may miss how **paralinguistic signals** can drive multi-turn drift/over-trust effects (e.g., user tone as a hidden confound).
