# WavBench: Benchmarking Reasoning, Colloquialism, and Paralinguistics for End-to-End Spoken Dialogue Models

- Year: 2026
- Venue: arXiv
- Authors: Yangzhuo Li; Shengpeng Ji; Yifu Chen; Tianle Liang; Haorong Ying; Yule Wang; Junbo Li; Jun Fang; Zhou Zhao
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12135
- BibTeX key (if we add it): wavbench2026li
- Tags: spoken-dialogue, multi-turn, benchmark, paralinguistics, robustness, evaluation

## One-sentence takeaway

WavBench is a multi-faceted benchmark for end-to-end spoken dialogue models that jointly stresses (i) harder reasoning, (ii) “listenable” colloquial dialogue quality, and (iii) paralinguistic/acoustic understanding and generation.

## What problem does it solve?

- Existing spoken-dialogue evaluations often inherit text-generation norms and under-measure audio-native aspects (emotion/prosody/volume/speaking rate/pitch, background sound handling) and colloquial “naturalness,” especially in multi-turn settings.
- Newer end-to-end speech dialogue models increasingly integrate reasoning, but benchmarks don’t simultaneously test reasoning + colloquial delivery + paralinguistic fidelity.

## What is the core method / protocol?

- Introduces **WavBench**, organized as a **tripartite** benchmark:
  - **Pro subset**: higher-difficulty tasks aimed at challenging reasoning-enhanced spoken dialogue models.
  - **Basic subset**: evaluates spoken **colloquialism** with an emphasis on “listenability” (natural vocabulary, linguistic fluency, interactive rapport) rather than purely written-form correctness.
  - **Acoustic subset**: evaluates paralinguistic competence across (a) explicit understanding, (b) explicit generation, and (c) **implicit dialogue** behaviors involving paralinguistic cues.
- Evaluates **five** state-of-the-art end-to-end spoken dialogue models (details not extracted in this rapid pass).

## What are the key metrics?

- The paper frames metrics along three axes (not all metric definitions extracted in this rapid pass):
  - **Reasoning / problem-solving** performance (esp. in Pro subset)
  - **Colloquial expression / listenability** (Basic/Pro)
  - **Paralinguistic / acoustic fidelity**: emotion/prosody-related understanding and generation; handling acoustic characteristics and background sounds.

## What are the main results?

- Across the five tested models, WavBench surfaces tradeoffs between:
  - reasoning ability,
  - colloquial delivery,
  - and paralinguistic fidelity.
- The intro/figures emphasize that **end-to-end** models show advantages in using paralinguistic information, and that gaps remain versus more structured/cascaded approaches for some interaction qualities.

## How is this similar to GALILEO?

- Shared theme: **robust evaluation under multi-turn interaction**, including failures that emerge only when dialogue context and interaction dynamics matter.
- Methodological neighbor for “benchmark design”: WavBench’s decomposition into subsets parallels how we might partition GALILEO-style evaluations into stressor categories.

## How is this different from GALILEO?

- WavBench focuses on **spoken/audio** dialogue (paralinguistics + colloquial speech) rather than text-only multi-turn drift/sycophancy/pressure dynamics.
- Primary axes are **speech naturalness** and **acoustic fidelity**, not (e.g.) belief drift, sycophancy, or truthfulness under pressure.

## Where GALILEO is stronger / cleaner (if true)

- If GALILEO is text-based: easier to control confounds and isolate **contextual pressure / drift** mechanisms without audio modality noise.
- GALILEO-style protocols can more directly target **stance/belief stability** and “agreement under pressure,” which are only indirectly related to WavBench.

## Where GALILEO is weaker / needs to improve

- Limited coverage of **audio-native** interaction risks and robustness issues (emotion/prosody/turn-taking cues), which are increasingly important for deployed assistants.
- If GALILEO expands to speech agents, it will need explicit paralinguistic evaluation dimensions like those operationalized here.
