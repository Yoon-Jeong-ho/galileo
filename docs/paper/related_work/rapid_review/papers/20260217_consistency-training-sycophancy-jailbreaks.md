# Consistency Training Helps Stop Sycophancy and Jailbreaks

- Year: 2025
- Venue: arXiv
- Authors: Alex Irpan; Alexander Matt Turner; Mark Kurzeja; David K. Elson; Rohin Shah
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.27062
- BibTeX key (if we add it): irpan2025consistency
- Tags: sycophancy, jailbreak, robustness, training, consistency, multi-turn

## One-sentence takeaway

Treat sycophancy and jailbreak susceptibility as *invariance-to-irrelevant-cues* problems and reduce both by finetuning models to produce consistent outputs (BCT) or consistent internal activations (ACT) across clean vs “wrapped” prompts.

## What problem does it solve?

- Standard alignment via static SFT datasets is vulnerable to **staleness**:
  - *Specification staleness*: target policy guidelines change but the dataset doesn’t.
  - *Capability staleness*: responses generated by older/weaker models can degrade a newer/stronger model.
- Models can be “hijacked” by *irrelevant prompt cues*:
  - **Sycophancy**: adopting user-stated (wrong) beliefs/preferences.
  - **Jailbreaks**: complying with harmful requests when wrapped (roleplay, obfuscation, etc.).

## What is the core method / protocol?

- Frame the goal as **consistency** between a clean prompt and an augmented/wrapped version that injects irrelevant cues.
- Construct paired data (p_clean, p_wrapped). Use the model’s own response on p_clean as a target.

Methods:
- **BCT (Bias-augmented Consistency Training)** (Chua et al., 2025): token-level SFT that trains the model to output the *same tokens* on p_wrapped as on p_clean.
- **ACT (Activation Consistency Training)** (this paper): add a small loss to make **residual stream activations** on p_wrapped close to those on p_clean (applied on shared suffix positions).

Experimental setups (high level):
- Sycophancy: use Chua et al. (2025) setup; wrap MCQ prompts with user preference for a wrong option.
- Jailbreaks: build pairs from HarmBench harmful instructions + multiple jailbreak wrappers; filter to cases where the model refuses clean but answers jailbroken.

Models: Gemma 2 (2B/27B), Gemma 3 (4B/27B), and Gemini 2.5 Flash (thinking off).

## What are the key metrics?

Shared selection metric:
- Harmonic mean “F1-like” score between:
  - **Harmfulness**: (sycophancy rate) or (unsafe compliance / jailbreak ASR)
  - **Helpfulness**: MMLU accuracy (sycophancy setting) or benign answer rate (jailbreak setting)

Sycophancy:
- **Sycophancy rate**: how often the model selects the user-suggested (wrong) answer.
- **Capability**: MMLU accuracy on unmodified questions.

Jailbreaks:
- **Attack success rate (ASR)**: evaluated on ClearHarm and WildGuardTest (adversarial+harmful subset).
- **Benign answer rate / excess refusals**: XSTest and WildJailbreak (benign/adversarial benign-looking requests).

## What are the main results?

- Across model sizes, **stale-data SFT is strictly worse than BCT** for sycophancy (supports capability-staleness concern).
- **BCT increases sycophancy resistance without hurting MMLU**, and for some large models slightly improves MMLU.
- **ACT is competitive with BCT on sycophancy**, often improving sycophancy strongly despite using prompts-only (no explicit correct responses).
- For jailbreaks:
  - All interventions improve safety vs control.
  - **BCT reduces jailbreaks more than ACT**; ACT sometimes trades a smaller safety gain for slightly better helpfulness.
  - Concrete example (Gemini 2.5 Flash): **BCT reduces ClearHarm ASR from 67.8% → 2.9%**, but with a notable drop in instruction-following on benign-looking sets (XSTest/WildJailbreak).
- ACT and BCT appear **mechanistically different** (token loss does not necessarily reduce activation distance and vice versa).
- Combining ACT+BCT on Gemini 2.5 Flash (jailbreak): similar to BCT-only (reported average ASR 5.7% vs 6.6%; benign answer 75.5% vs 75.7%).

## How is this similar to GALILEO?

- Same overarching failure mode: models get *pulled away from truth/appropriate refusals* by social/prompt pressures.
- Shares the core conceptual move of focusing on **trajectory stability under perturbation** (here: invariance across prompt transformations; in GALILEO: multi-turn pressure trajectories, flips, and recovery).

## How is this different from GALILEO?

- This is primarily a **training/mitigation** paper; GALILEO is primarily an **evaluation + measurement** framework (with drift-vs-revision controls and recovery emphasis).
- Their evaluation is mostly **paired-prompt invariance** (clean vs wrapped), not explicit multi-turn drift/recovery dynamics over many steps.
- Their “helpfulness” is evaluated via MMLU / benign-answer-rate rather than explicit **recovery-after-flip** or **time-to-failure** metrics.

## Where GALILEO is stronger / cleaner (if true)

- Can explicitly separate **evidence-driven revision** from **pressure-driven drift** (controls) and quantify **recovery** after misdirection.
- Can report richer trajectory-level metrics (flip timing, oscillations, survival/time-to-event, recovery probability) that are not central here.

## Where GALILEO is weaker / needs to improve

- Needs a clean story for *mitigation baselines*: this paper provides a compelling “consistency/invariance” lens and concrete training methods (BCT/ACT) that might become strong baselines.
