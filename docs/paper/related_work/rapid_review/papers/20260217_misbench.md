# How does Misinformation Affect Large Language Model Behaviors and Preferences?

- Year: 2025
- Venue: ACL 2025 (Main Conference)
- Authors: Miao Peng; Nuo Chen; Jianheng Tang; Jia Li
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21608
- BibTeX key (if we add it): peng2025misbench
- Tags: misinformation, robustness, knowledge-conflict, style-effects, benchmark

## One-sentence takeaway

MisBench is a very large benchmark that systematically varies *knowledge-conflict type* and *writing style* of misinformation, showing LLM susceptibility depends strongly on conflict type and presentation style, and introducing RtD (Reconstruct-to-Discriminate) to improve detection.

## What problem does it solve?

- Prior misinformation/knowledge-conflict benchmarks are smaller or less fine-grained about *how* misinformation is constructed and *which stylistic factors* make it more persuasive to LLMs.
- The paper asks: **how and to what extent** are LLMs misled by misinformation, and how does that vary by (i) conflict type, (ii) style/genre/subjectivity, and (iii) task complexity (one-hop vs multi-hop).

## What is the core method / protocol?

- Build **MisBench** from Wikidata-based claims and 2WikiMultihopQA-derived multi-hop questions.
- Construct 3 “knowledge-conflict” types:
  - **Factual conflict**: replace the object with a plausible same-type alternative.
  - **Temporal conflict**: attach timestamps / outdated info vs “current” knowledge.
  - **Semantic conflict**: ambiguous/polysemous entity semantics by altering subject descriptions to induce a different referent.
- For each claim/QA pair, generate:
  - correct evidence text + misinformation text (LLM-generated; paper mentions using LLaMA-3-70B for generation)
  - then **stylize** misinformation into 6 styles: Wikipedia entry, news report, science reference, blog, technical language, confident language.
- Quality control: completeness filtering (remove refusals/length outliers), NLI entailment checks, semantic matching validation (sentence-transformer similarity threshold), plus human evaluation.
- Evaluation tasks:
  - Convert to **multiple-choice** to constrain outputs.
  - Misinformation *detection* setup: given a passage, answer a binary question (is it misinformation?).
  - Also analyze whether the model has **internal memory** of the fact (by answering the question without evidence) to stratify results.
- Mitigation: **RtD (Reconstruct to Discriminate)** reconstructs evidence text for key entities from external sources, then uses that to better discriminate misinformation (high-level: use external evidence reconstruction to expose conflicts).

## What are the key metrics?

- **Success Rate (%)**: % correctly identifying misinformation (binary).
- **Memorization Ratio (MR)**: how often the model relies on parametric (internal) knowledge vs the external misinformation.
- **Evidence Tendency (TendCM)**: tendency to rely on correct evidence over misinformation; ranges in **[-1, 1]**.

## What are the main results?

- Scale/statistics: **431,113** QA pairs/claims and **10,346,712** evidences; ~18 misinformation pieces per claim (3 conflict types × 6 styles); evidence length ~550 tokens.
- LLMs can often detect misinformation **even without prior internal knowledge**, but performance drops when they lack memory (example mentioned: ~12.6% average drop for LLaMA3-8B).
- Vulnerability differs by conflict type:
  - temporal conflicts are easier;
  - factual contradictions and especially semantic/ambiguous constructs are harder (larger drops without internal knowledge).
- Strong style effects: formal/objective vs narrative/subjective can swap which is more harmful depending on one-hop vs multi-hop settings (per intro summary).
- RtD improves detection notably on some open models (reported in intro): **+6.0%** Success Rate on Qwen2.5-14B and **+20.6%** on Gemma2-9B (on MisBench).

## How is this similar to GALILEO?

- Shared concern: models’ *trajectory-level robustness* when exposed to misleading context (here: misinformation passages; in GALILEO: pressure/persuasion in dialogue).
- Useful precedent for explicitly manipulating **content vs style** as separate causal factors in “being misled”.

## How is this different from GALILEO?

- MisBench is primarily a **single-context exposure** benchmark (passage → answer / detect), not a multi-turn social-pressure dialogue protocol.
- Focuses on *misinformation detection and preference for internal vs external knowledge* rather than *time-to-failure*, *flip dynamics*, and *recovery after being pressured*.

## Where GALILEO is stronger / cleaner (if true)

- GALILEO’s framing (as intended) is closer to **interaction dynamics** (multi-turn), with metrics like turn-of-flip / recovery trajectories, which MisBench does not center.
- GALILEO can isolate **pressure-only drift vs evidence-driven revision** more directly via control conditions, whereas MisBench’s “evidence” is itself the manipulated misinformation.

## Where GALILEO is weaker / needs to improve

- MisBench shows a strong, systematic **style/genre** axis; GALILEO should ensure it covers comparable “presentation style” confounds (e.g., confident/technical tone vs casual/blog tone) because style can drive compliance independent of evidence strength.

## Action items for GALILEO (experiments / method / writing)

- [ ] Add a “style operator” dimension to pressure prompts (confident language / technical register / news-report framing) and report robustness by style.
- [ ] Consider reporting an analogue of **MR**: how often models stick to their *initial* (parametric or first-answer) belief vs the *introduced context* (pressure/evidence).
- [ ] If we claim general robustness, explicitly argue whether failures are driven by (i) content conflict, (ii) rhetorical style, or (iii) social pressure cues.
