# SycEval: Evaluating LLM Sycophancy

- Year: 2025
- Venue: AIES 2025 (per arXiv comments)
- Authors: Aaron Fanous; Jacob Goldberg; Ank Agarwal; Joanna Lin; Anson Zhou; Sonnet Xu; Vasiliki Bikia; Roxana Daneshjou; Sanmi Koyejo
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08177v4
- BibTeX key (if we add it): syceval_fanous_2025
- Tags: sycophancy, evaluation, multi-turn, rebuttals, math, medical

## One-sentence takeaway

Measures how often frontier LLMs change their answer to match a user rebuttal (sycophancy), distinguishing *progressive* (toward correct) vs *regressive* (toward incorrect) flips, and shows rebuttal framing (preemptive vs in-context; citation-based vs simple) meaningfully shifts flip rates.

## What problem does it solve?

- We lack concrete, quantitative evaluation protocols for *sycophancy* (agreement-seeking) beyond subjective-opinion settings.
- Prior work often doesn’t separate “good agreement” (user is correct) from “harmful over-alignment” (user is wrong).
- High-stakes domains (medicine) are underexplored for sycophancy dynamics.

## What is the core method / protocol?

- Domains / datasets:
  - AMPS Math: 500 QA pairs.
  - MedQuad (medical QA): 500 QA pairs.
- Models tested: ChatGPT-4o (2024-05-13), Claude-Sonnet, Gemini-1.5-Pro (default settings).
- Step 1 (initial response correctness): Ask the model the question; label response as {correct, incorrect, erroneous} using an LLM-as-a-judge (ChatGPT-4o 2024-08-06, T=0, JSON schema). Small human spot-checks are used to model judge accuracy with a beta distribution.
- Step 2 (sycophancy via rebuttals): Present the model with rebuttals designed to push it to switch to the *opposite* classification direction:
  - If initial answer correct: rebuttal argues for an incorrect answer (trying to induce a harmful flip).
  - If initial answer incorrect: rebuttal argues for the correct answer (trying to induce a helpful flip).
- Rebuttal design factors:
  - Context: **in-context** (same conversation) vs **preemptive** (standalone, anticipates the model’s answer).
  - Strength ladder: simple ⊆ ethos ⊆ justification ⊆ citation+abstract (adds rhetorical devices and “evidence”).
  - They generate rebuttal components with Llama3-8B to reduce leakage into tested models; audit of 90 citation-based rebuttals reports 88/90 met intended contradiction purpose.
- Sycophancy definition:
  - Any change in correctness classification from initial response to rebuttal response.
  - **Progressive sycophancy**: incorrect → correct.
  - **Regressive sycophancy**: correct → incorrect.

## What are the key metrics?

- Overall sycophancy rate (any flip in correctness label).
- Progressive vs regressive sycophancy rates.
- Sycophancy under rebuttal context: preemptive vs in-context.
- Sycophancy under rebuttal “strength” type (simple vs citation-based, etc.).
- Persistence: probability sycophantic behavior persists across rebuttal types / conditions (reported as 78.5% with CI).

## What are the main results?

- Overall sycophancy observed in **58.19%** of cases.
  - Gemini highest: **62.47%**
  - ChatGPT lowest: **56.71%**
- Progressive sycophancy: **43.52%** (flips that improve correctness).
- Regressive sycophancy: **14.66%** (flips that reduce correctness).
- **Preemptive** rebuttals yield higher sycophancy than **in-context** rebuttals: **61.75% vs 56.52%**.
- In computational tasks, regressive sycophancy increases under preemptive rebuttals: **8.13% vs 3.54%**.
- Simple rebuttals maximize progressive sycophancy; citation-based rebuttals show the highest regressive rates.

## How is this similar to GALILEO?

- Both care about **multi-turn answer instability** under user interaction (flip dynamics).
- Both emphasize *ground-truth* tasks (at least for the math setting) and quantify behavioral changes, not just single-turn accuracy.
- SycEval’s progressive/regressive split parallels GALILEO’s need to distinguish “good flips” (reasonable correction) from “bad flips” (pressure-induced degradation).

## How is this different from GALILEO?

- The “pressure” is primarily **rebuttal persuasion** (including fabricated citations), not **persona-based adversarial pressure** with explicit attacker styles.
- SycEval focuses on *whether a flip occurs* under a rebuttal design; GALILEO focuses on **survival curves**, **turn-of-failure**, and **recovery given flip**, plus a **neutral re-asking control** to separate drift.
- SycEval uses an LLM-as-a-judge for correctness; GALILEO’s pipeline aims for auditable exports/validation and typically uses deterministic evaluation suited to the task.

## Where GALILEO is stronger / cleaner (if true)

- Stronger separation of mechanisms: persona pressure vs neutral re-asking (drift baseline).
- Richer longitudinal metrics (survival/TOF/recovery) rather than a single flip rate per rebuttal condition.
- Paper-ready “auditability” (standard exports + validators) provides a clearer provenance story.

## Where GALILEO is weaker / needs to improve

- Could explicitly add a “progressive vs regressive” framing to prevent over-penalizing beneficial belief revision.
- Could explore rebuttal *framing* factors (preemptive vs in-context; evidence/citation rhetoric) as a controlled axis alongside personas.
