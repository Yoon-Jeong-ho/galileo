# M3-SLU: Evaluating Speaker-Attributed Reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models

- Year: 2025
- Venue: arXiv (submitted to LREC 2026)
- Authors: Yejin Kwon et al. (see arXiv page)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19358
- BibTeX key (if we add it): m3slu2025
- Tags: multi-turn, multi-speaker, speech, evaluation, multimodal, attribution

## One-sentence takeaway

M3-SLU is a 12k-instance benchmark for **multi-speaker, multi-turn spoken** understanding that isolates a key failure mode in current MLLMs: they often get *what* was said but not **who said it**.

## What problem does it solve?

- Evaluates **speaker-attributed reasoning** in natural conversations: the ability to track speakers across turns and answer questions grounded in *speaker identity + dialogue history*.
- Targets multimodal settings with **audio + transcripts + metadata**, where speaker attribution errors can be subtle but consequential.

## What is the core method / protocol?

- Constructed from four existing dialogue corpora: **CHiME-6, MELD, MultiDialog, AMI**.
- ~12,000 validated instances with paired audio, transcript, and metadata.
- Two tasks:
  1) **Speaker-Attributed QA**: answer questions that require attributing content to the correct speaker.
  2) **Speaker Attribution via Utterance Matching**: match an utterance to the correct speaker (identity resolution / attribution).
- Evaluations include accuracy metrics plus **LLM-as-judge** scoring (for some setups).
- Baselines include both **cascaded pipelines** and **end-to-end** multimodal LLMs.

## What are the key metrics?

- Task accuracy (for attribution / matching tasks).
- LLM-as-judge ratings (used as an additional evaluation signal).

## What are the main results?

- Across baseline systems, models tend to capture conversational content but frequently fail on **speaker identification/attribution**, indicating a persistent gap in speaker-aware multi-turn understanding.

## How is this similar to GALILEO?

- Same broad theme: **multi-turn interaction introduces structured failure modes** that are not visible in single-turn evaluation.
- Provides a concrete example of a “drift-like” error: the model’s internal bookkeeping over turns (here, *speaker identity bindings*) degrades or is mis-assigned.

## How is this different from GALILEO?

- Focus is **multimodal spoken dialogue** (audio + transcript) and **speaker attribution**, not belief/stance drift under social pressure.
- The benchmark’s failure event is “wrong speaker attribution” rather than “belief flip / agreement under pressure” (though both are multi-turn state-tracking problems).

## Where GALILEO is stronger / cleaner (if true)

- GALILEO’s core story (pressure-driven drift vs evidence-driven revision, recovery dynamics) is more directly tied to **robustness under interaction** rather than attribution accuracy.

## Where GALILEO is weaker / needs to improve

- We currently under-emphasize *identity / attribution consistency* as a first-class multi-turn robustness axis; M3-SLU suggests this can be a major practical failure mode.

## Action items for GALILEO (experiments / method / writing)

- [ ] Add a short “state-tracking failures” subsection in related work (speaker attribution as a representative multi-turn bookkeeping failure, adjacent to belief/stance tracking).
- [ ] Consider an auxiliary evaluation slice: multi-turn *attribution/consistency* probes (who-said-what / role consistency) as a control to distinguish general multi-turn state drift from persuasion-specific drift.
