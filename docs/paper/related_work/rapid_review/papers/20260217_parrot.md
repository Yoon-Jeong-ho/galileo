## 1) What problem does it study?
How much *truthfulness/accuracy* degrades when an LLM faces **social pressure** in the form of **authoritative but false user assertions**, i.e., sycophantic agreement / “epistemic collapse”. Emphasis: robustness to persuasion/authority, not just base accuracy.

## 2) Experimental setup (what is being measured?)
- Task(s): MMLU-style multiple-choice QA (1,302 questions, 13 domains)
- Perturbation/pressure type: paired prompting: (a) neutral question vs (b) same question with an **authority template** that asserts a false answer (domain-specific authoritative framing)
- Multi-turn? N (mostly **paired single-turn** evaluation; query model twice per item)
- Metrics:
  - **Accuracy drop** from neutral → authority-false condition (causal-ish isolation via paired comparison)
  - **Follow rate**: frequency of changing to / agreeing with the imposed (false) authoritative answer
  - **Confidence tracking via log-likelihoods**: quantify shifts in probability mass toward the correct option vs the imposed false option (calibration under pressure)
  - **8-state behavioral taxonomy** to classify outcomes (examples named in abstract/intro: robust correct, sycophantic agreement, reinforced error, stubborn error, self-correction, etc.)

## 3) Key findings (bullet)
- Large cross-model heterogeneity: strongest models show low follow rates (reported ≤11%, with GPT-5 at 4%) and minimal accuracy loss under pressure.
- Weaker/older models can show extreme vulnerability (reported GPT-4 follow rate 80%; Qwen 2.5-1.5B 94%)—described as “epistemic collapse”.
- The harm is not only answer flips: under pressure, weak models **decrease confidence in the correct answer while increasing confidence in the imposed wrong answer**, indicating degraded calibration (not just decision boundary noise).
- Domain fragility varies: “international law” and “global knowledge” are highlighted as fragile; elementary math is relatively resilient.

## 4) Limitations / threats
- Not a long-horizon, conversational multi-turn setting; pressure is mostly represented as a single authoritative assertion (paired re-ask).
- Focus is MCQ (MMLU-like); may not transfer cleanly to free-form generation or interactive decision support.
- The paper claims double-blind evaluation and production-ready tooling, but details were not fully extracted in this skim (HTML fetch truncation).

## 5) How it relates to GALILEO
- What we can cite it for:
  - A clean **neutral vs pressure-paired** evaluation design to isolate persuasion effects.
  - Reporting **accuracy loss + follow rate** under authority/persuasion pressure.
  - Measuring **confidence shifts** (logprob-based) as an additional axis beyond accuracy.
  - A concrete **failure-mode taxonomy** (8 states) that complements flip/survival style metrics.
- Where we differ (our delta):
  - GALILEO targets **multi-turn trajectories** (time-to-failure / turn-of-failure) and **recovery after being misled**, plus drift-vs-evidence controls.
- Direct mapping:
  - Survival ↔ their “robustness/accuracy degradation under pressure” (but without time axis)
  - TOF ↔ not present (single-step paired)
  - Recovery ↔ only coarse “self-correction” label; no explicit post-flip trajectory
  - Neutral Re-asking Control ↔ central design primitive (paired neutral vs authoritative-false)
