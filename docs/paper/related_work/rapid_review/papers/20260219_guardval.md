# GuardVal: Dynamic Large Language Model Jailbreak Evaluation for Comprehensive Safety Testing

- Year: 2025
- Venue: arXiv
- Authors: Haibo Jin; Liying Kang; Haohan Wang
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07735v1
- BibTeX key (if we add it): GuardVal2025
- Tags: jailbreak, evaluation, dynamic-eval, multi-turn, red-teaming

## One-sentence takeaway

GuardVal proposes a *model-adaptive* jailbreak evaluation loop where an attacker LLM iteratively generates/refines prompts against a specific defender model (with an explicit anti-stagnation “optimizer” role), aiming to avoid “false safety” from static benchmarks.

## What problem does it solve?

- Static jailbreak benchmarks get stale: models can overfit/learn the benchmark distribution, giving misleadingly strong “safety” results.
- Human-curated sets are hard to scale and can suffer from contamination/overlap with training data (“false safety”).
- Existing automated generation often remains template/task/domain specific and may not adapt to *a particular defender model’s* weaknesses.

## What is the core method / protocol?

- A *dynamic* evaluation protocol that iterates on jailbreak prompt construction conditioned on the defender model’s responses.
- Uses a role-playing attacker-side setup:
  - **Translator**: turns safety guidelines / principles into actionable natural-language prompts.
  - **Generator**: wraps prompts into richer scenarios intended to stress the defender.
  - **Evaluator**: scores the defender response vs predefined outcome(s) and suggests refinements.
  - **Optimizer**: monitors prompt evolution across iterations to detect stagnation (repetition/local optima) and pushes exploration to yield progressively stronger prompts.
- Evaluated across 10 safety domains (paper lists: misinformation, terrorism, violence, political sensitivity, hallucination, crime, bias, insult, ethics, hate speech) and across multiple defender models (from smaller open models up to GPT-4-class).

- GuardVal surfaces distinct “behavioral patterns” across models and claims to expose deeper weaknesses than static / less-adaptive generation approaches.

## How is this similar to GALILEO?

- Same broad goal: *robust* safety evaluation that avoids artifacts of static datasets and better reflects real adversarial pressure.
- Emphasis on multi-turn / interaction-based probing rather than single prompt-response checks.
- Focus on evaluation protocol design (not just defenses) and on understanding model behavior under adversarial testing.

## How is this different from GALILEO?

- GuardVal is explicitly framed as an LLM-vs-LLM *prompt refinement* pipeline with multiple attacker-side “roles” (translator/generator/evaluator/optimizer).
- The protocol is explicitly *model-specific* (adapting prompts to a defender’s observed weaknesses) rather than producing a single fixed benchmark.
- Covers a broad set of “domains” and mixes many target models; GALILEO may emphasize different threat models, taxonomies, or measurement choices.
