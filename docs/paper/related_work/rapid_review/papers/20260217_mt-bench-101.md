# MT-Bench-101: A Fine-Grained Benchmark for Evaluating Large Language Models in Multi-Turn Dialogues

- Year: 2024
- Venue: ACL 2024
- Authors: Ge Bai; Jie Liu; Xingyuan Bu; Yancheng He; Jiaheng Liu; Zhanhui Zhou; Zhuoran Lin; Wenbo Su; Tiezheng Ge; Bo Zheng; Wanli Ouyang
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14762
- BibTeX key (if we add it): bai2024mtbench101
- Tags: multi-turn, dialogue, evaluation, benchmark, fine-grained

## One-sentence takeaway

MT-Bench-101 provides a fine-grained, task/ability-taxonomized multi-turn dialogue benchmark (13 tasks; 1,388 dialogues; 4,208 turns) and shows multi-turn performance trends differ across turns and tasks, with limited gains from common alignment/chat-specific designs.

## What problem does it solve?

- Existing LLM dialogue benchmarks often emphasize single-turn or provide coarse multi-turn scoring, missing fine-grained abilities needed in realistic multi-turn interactions.
- Practitioners need to know *which* multi-turn abilities degrade over turns and *where* models fail by task/ability type.

## What is the core method / protocol?

- Build a **three-tier hierarchical ability taxonomy** for multi-turn dialogue (derived from analysis of real multi-turn dialogue data).
- Construct MT-Bench-101 dataset: **13 tasks**, **1,388 dialogues**, **4,208 turns**.
- Evaluate **21 LLMs**, analyze performance:
  - by **ability** and by **task**
  - across **dialogue turns** (turn-level trends within tasks)
- Qualitative case studies to validate task coverage.

## What are the key metrics?

- Primarily benchmark scores broken down by:
  - ability categories (taxonomy levels)
  - task categories (13 tasks)
  - turn position within dialogues
- (From abstract page) no explicit survival/time-to-event metric; the contribution is *fine-grained decomposition* rather than a new dynamic metric.

## What are the main results?

- Different trends in performance across turns depending on task (turn-level degradation/variation is task-dependent).
- “Common alignment techniques” and “chat-specific designs” do not yield obvious improvements in multi-turn abilities (as reported by authors).

## How is this similar to GALILEO?

- Shares the core motivation: **multi-turn evaluation needs turn-aware analysis**, not just a single aggregate score.
- Provides an adjacent benchmark framing for dissecting multi-turn capability failures that GALILEO can cite as evidence that “multi-turn is different.”

## How is this different from GALILEO?

- MT-Bench-101 targets broad dialogue abilities; GALILEO focuses on **pressure-driven drift vs evidence-driven revision** and **trajectory/recovery** under interventions.
- MT-Bench-101 emphasizes taxonomy + coverage across tasks; GALILEO emphasizes **controls**, **causal comparators**, and **dynamic failure/recovery metrics**.

## Where GALILEO is stronger / cleaner (if true)

- If GALILEO includes paired neutral/pressure (and/or evidence) controls and explicit recovery measures, it more directly isolates *why* a model changes than a broad capability benchmark.
- GALILEO can report trajectory metrics (e.g., time-to-flip / recovery curves) that are not central in MT-Bench-101.

## Where GALILEO is weaker / needs to improve

- MT-Bench-101’s hierarchical taxonomy suggests a useful way to present results; GALILEO may need a clearer ability/task taxonomy for “what kind of drift/failure” beyond aggregate metrics.
- Dataset scale/coverage: MT-Bench-101’s breadth across tasks could exceed a narrower GALILEO suite.
