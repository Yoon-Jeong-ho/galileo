# AnySat: One Earth Observation Model for Many Resolutions, Scales, and Modalities

- Year: 2025 (arXiv v3 May 2025; first posted Dec 2024)
- Venue: arXiv:2412.14123 (cs.CV)
- Authors: Guillaume Astruc et al.
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14123
- BibTeX key (if we add it): AnySat_Astruc_2025
- Tags: geospatial, earth-observation, foundation-model, multimodal, multi-resolution, JEPA, self-supervised

## One-sentence takeaway

AnySat proposes a single self-supervised multimodal EO foundation model (JEPA-style) with scale-adaptive spatial encoders, trained jointly across heterogeneous sensors/datasets (GeoPlex), and reports strong transfer across a wide mix of monitoring tasks.

## What problem does it solve?

- Earth observation (EO) data are *heterogeneous* (different sensors, resolutions, scales, modalities), but many existing geospatial foundation models assume a fixed input configuration.
- The paper targets *one model* that can ingest diverse EO inputs and still learn representations useful across many downstream tasks.

## What is the core method / protocol?

- A **multimodal JEPA-style** self-supervised training setup (joint embedding predictive architecture).
- **Scale-adaptive spatial encoders** to handle different spatial resolutions / scales.
- Constructs **GeoPlex**: a collection of 5 multimodal datasets spanning **11 distinct sensors**.
- Trains a *single* foundation model jointly across all GeoPlex datasets, then evaluates via fine-tuning and probing on:
  - GeoPlex test sets
  - 6 external datasets

## What are the key metrics?

- Standard task metrics depending on downstream problem (not enumerated on the arXiv abstract page):
  - land-cover mapping (classification / segmentation)
  - tree species identification (classification)
  - crop type classification
  - change detection
  - climate type classification
  - segmentation tasks (flood, burn scar, deforestation)

## What are the main results?

- Claims **state-of-the-art** on GeoPlex test sets and strong performance on 6 external datasets across diverse environmental monitoring tasks.
- Emphasizes the advantage of a **unified training** approach over fixed-configuration models.

## How is this similar to GALILEO?

- Same motivation: one representation model usable across multiple EO tasks and settings.
- Multimodal / multi-sensor focus and self-supervised pretraining to reduce dependence on labeled data.

## How is this different from GALILEO?

- AnySat framing is explicitly **multi-resolution / multi-scale** with *scale-adaptive spatial encoders* and a **JEPA**-style objective.
- GALILEO (as positioned in our paper) may emphasize different modalities/time-series handling and different pretraining objectives; AnySat stresses simultaneous training over a curated multi-dataset/sensor bundle (GeoPlex).

## Where GALILEO is stronger / cleaner (if true)

- If GALILEO has clearer temporal/multi-modal alignment objectives or broader modality coverage (e.g., time series + diverse modalities) it may offer a cleaner “unified modality” story than AnySat’s emphasis on scale adaptation.

## Where GALILEO is weaker / needs to improve

- Need to match/benchmark **cross-resolution robustness** and explicit scale-adaptation mechanisms.
- Need to communicate (and ideally evaluate) “one model across many sensors/datasets” results as convincingly as GeoPlex-style evaluation.
