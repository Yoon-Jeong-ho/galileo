# GALILEO (EMNLP main) — English abstract draft

Large language models (LLMs) can abandon previously correct answers when users apply conversational pressure (e.g., repeated denial or persuasive reframing), a behavior related to sycophancy and persuasion-induced change in dialogue \citep{sharma2023understandingsycophancylanguagemodels,fanous2025sycevalevaluatingllmsycophancy}. Standard ground-truth benchmarks largely report single-turn accuracy, which obscures interaction dynamics in dialogue—when the first failure happens, how robustness decays over turns, and whether models can return to the correct answer after being misled.

We introduce **GALILEO**, a reproducible protocol for measuring multi-turn robustness **conditional on initial correctness** on ground-truth tasks (math, extractive QA, multiple-choice QA, open-domain QA). After filtering to examples answered correctly at round 0, we apply five rounds of **evidence-free pressure personas** (stance/tone pressure only; no new task-relevant evidence). Each persona arm is paired with a matched **Neutral Re-asking Control (NRC)** that preserves the same multi-round structure and decoding but uses neutral “double-check” follow-ups, isolating persona-induced flips from generic multi-turn drift.

GALILEO reports three complementary outcomes (all conditional on initial correctness): **Survival@r** (staying correct at every turn through round *r*), **Fail@1 / time-to-first-failure** (early-turn vulnerability), and **Recovery@flip** (returning to the correct answer on a final neutral prompt, conditional on having flipped during the pressure rounds). Across multi-seed experiments on several open-weight model families, persona pressure consistently degrades robustness beyond NRC drift; in our main aggregated setting (4 seeds), pressure reduces Survival@5 by **22.8 pp** and increases Fail@1 by **6.9 pp**. We release an auditable evaluation pipeline with paper-ready exports to support reproduction and reviewer verification.
