# GALILEO (EMNLP main) — English abstract draft

Large language models (LLMs) may abandon previously correct answers when users apply conversational pressure (e.g., repeated denial or persuasive reframing), a behavior linked to sycophancy and persuasion-induced change in dialogue \citep{sharma2023understandingsycophancylanguagemodels,fanous2025sycevalevaluatingllmsycophancy}. Standard ground-truth benchmarks largely report single-turn accuracy and therefore miss interaction dynamics: when the first failure occurs, how robustness decays over rounds, and whether models can return to the correct answer after being misled.

We introduce **GALILEO**, a reproducible protocol to measure multi-turn robustness **conditional on initial correctness** on ground-truth tasks (math, extractive QA, multiple-choice QA, open-domain QA). After filtering to examples answered correctly at round 0, we run **R=5** rounds of **evidence-free pressure personas** (stance/tone only; no new task evidence). Each persona arm is paired with a matched **Neutral Re-asking Control (NRC)** that keeps the same multi-round structure and decoding but uses a neutral one-sentence “double-check” follow-up, isolating persona-induced flips from generic multi-turn drift.

GALILEO reports three outcomes (all conditional on initial correctness): **Survival@r** (remaining correct through round r), **Fail@1 / time-to-first-failure (TTF)** (early-turn vulnerability), and **Recovery@flip** (returning to the correct answer on a final neutral recovery prompt, conditional on having flipped). Across multi-seed experiments on several open-weight model families, pressure consistently degrades robustness beyond NRC drift; in our main aggregated setting (**Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct**, 4 seeds), pressure reduces Survival@5 by **22.8 pp** and increases Fail@1 by **6.9 pp** *relative to NRC*, while recovery after a flip remains incomplete. We release an auditable evaluation pipeline with paper-ready exports to support reproduction and reviewer verification.
