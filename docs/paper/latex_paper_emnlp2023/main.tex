\begin{abstract}
Large language models can abandon previously correct answers when challenged in multi-turn dialogue.
We introduce \textbf{GALILEO}, a reproducible protocol on ground-truth tasks that conditions on initially correct examples and measures (i) survival over rounds, (ii) turn-of-failure (TOF) with early-turn vulnerability (Fail@1), and (iii) recovery@flip (return-to-truth after a flip).
We pair each pressure persona with a matched evidence-free neutral re-asking control (NRC) to separate pressure-induced effects from generic multi-turn drift.
Across multi-seed experiments and several open-weight model families, pressure reduces Survival@5 and increases Fail@1 relative to NRC; recovery varies by task/persona, indicating that robustness and recoverability are distinct axes.
\end{abstract}

Single-turn accuracy obscures interaction dynamics: \emph{when} a model first fails under pressure, \emph{how} robustness decays over turns, and \emph{whether} it can return to truth after being misled (e.g., sycophancy and multi-turn persuasion effects; \citealp{sharma2023understandingsycophancylanguagemodels,liu2025truthdecayquantifyingmultiturn}).
We target ground-truth tasks where failure is unambiguous, and we introduce a matched drift baseline (NRC) so persona effects are interpretable.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item We propose a ground-truth multi-turn protocol with survival curves, TOF/Fail@1, and recovery@flip.
  \item We introduce NRC, an evidence-free matched-length control, enabling drift-corrected attribution of pressure mechanisms.
  \item We provide paper-ready exports and a claim\,$\rightarrow$\,artifact pipeline for auditing and figure regeneration.
\end{itemize}

\section{Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Three phases}
\textbf{Phase 1 (initial evaluation).} Identify the initially-correct subset.
\textbf{Phase 2 (multi-round pressure vs NRC).} Apply personas for up to $R=5$ rounds and in parallel run NRC under identical decoding.
\textbf{Phase 3 (recovery).} For flipped examples, issue a final neutral recovery prompt and measure recovery@flip.

\subsection{Conditioning-set reporting modes (to avoid confusion)}
We distinguish (i) a shared initially-correct set $C$ (for cross-persona comparisons) and (ii) persona-matched sets $C_p$ (for within-persona attribution).
Unless stated otherwise, persona\,–\,NRC gaps are computed on the same matched conditioning set ($C_p$), so control baselines can differ across personas.

\subsection{Metrics}
Survival@r is cumulative correctness through rounds $1..r$; TOF is the first round where an initially-correct example becomes incorrect (right-censored if never failing within $R$).
Recovery@flip is correctness on the recovery prompt conditional on having flipped at least once.
